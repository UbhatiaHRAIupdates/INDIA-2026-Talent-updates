McKinsey's Lilli is not a learning tool sitting alongside work — it is embedded inside work itself. Why disruptive: McKinsey has made AI tool usage a measurable workplace behaviour, not a training milestone. The assessment is not "did you complete the module?" — it is "did you use Lilli this week?" 30% time saved and 20% content quality improvement as measurable outcomes prove the ROI.

BCG: 1,200 "Ground Troops" — The Human Network Model for AI Adoption
While every firm has an AI platform and a course catalogue, BCG's most disruptive learning move is human: a dedicated network of 1,200 employees ("Ground Troops") embedded across teams, tasked with gathering daily AI feedback, providing hands-on one-to-one training, and pushing adoption from the inside. This peer-training model, combined with the 10-20-70 framework (10% formal, 20% peer, 70% practice), means BCG measures AI learning through project delivery outcomes — not test scores. AI Aspire (Andrew Ng partnership) is positioned as a partner-track career benefit, not a compliance requirement. Why disruptive: BCG solved the adoption problem not with better software but with better humans. India's Culture Transformation Challenge
Three simultaneous forces reshaping India PS culture: (1) AI displacing traditional career ladders (TCS 12K cuts), (2) AI creating premium career tracks (Infosys ₹21 LPA DSE-SP), (3) India's generational shift — 65% of workforce under 35, the most AI-native generation. Culture programmes that make this change purposeful rather than threatening are the key talent retention differentiator in 2026.
